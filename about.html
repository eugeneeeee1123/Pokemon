Ancient Hisui and Kalos

Source: Legends: Arceus, X and Y

Early hand-crafted Poké Balls are documented in Hisui. Three thousand years before modern leagues, AZ fired the ultimate weapon in Kalos, establishing an event that later split the series into two branches.

Kanto and Hoenn beginnings

Source: Red, Green, Blue, Ruby, Sapphire

The original Kanto expedition and the Hoenn journey occur roughly concurrently. Team Rocket disbands in Kanto as ancient legendary Pokémon awaken in Hoenn.

Johto and Sinnoh league era

Source: Gold, Silver, Diamond, Pearl

Set three years after events in Kanto. The Johto journey deals with Rocket remnants while Team Galactic provokes space-time disturbances across Sinnoh.

Mega Evolution timeline split

Source: Omega Ruby, Alpha Sapphire Delta Episode

Dialogue during the Delta Episode establishes two distinct continuities: one where Mega Evolution was unlocked by the ultimate weapon, and an earlier branch where it never existed.

Legendary & Mythical Pokémon

Regional catalogue of Legendary and Mythical species across all nine generations, with canonical appearances across the mainline games and theatrical releases.

Kanto (Generation I)

Kanto established the first legendary trio with the elemental winged mirages Articuno, Zapdos, and Moltres, roosting in remote wilderness across the region. Genetic research conducted on Faraway Island led Team Rocket scientists to clone Mew into the psychic powerhouse Mewtwo in the Cinnabar Island laboratory.

Pokémon: Articuno · #144, Zapdos · #145, Moltres · #146, Mewtwo · #150, Mew · #151

Games: Red, Blue, Yellow, FireRed, LeafGreen, Let's Go, Pikachu! / Eevee!

Film & Media: Mewtwo Strikes Back, The Power of One, Mewtwo Returns

Johto (Generation II)

Johto folklore centers on the destruction of Ecruteak City's Brass Tower, where three unnamed Pokémon perished in the flames and were resurrected by Ho-Oh as the legendary beasts Raikou, Entei, and Suicune. Sky guardian Lugia nested in the Whirl Islands, while time-traveling guardian Celebi protects the ancient shrine deep inside Ilex Forest.

Pokémon: Raikou · #243, Entei · #244, Suicune · #245, Lugia · #249, Ho-Oh · #250, Celebi · #251

Games: Gold, Silver, Crystal, HeartGold, SoulSilver

Film & Media: The Power of One, Spell of the Unown, Celebi: Voice of the Forest, Zoroark: Master of Illusions

Hoenn (Generation III)

Hoenn's geography was shaped by primeval titan clashes between ancient ocean ruler Kyogre and landmass creator Groudon, kept in check by ozone layer sovereign Rayquaza. Sealed away in ancient stone ruins are the sealed titan trio Regirock, Regice, and Registeel, alongside roaming twin guardians Latias and Latios, wish-granting Jirachi, and extraterrestrial mutation Deoxys.

Pokémon: Regirock · #377, Regice · #378, Registeel · #379, Latias · #380, Latios · #381, Kyogre · #382, Groudon · #383, Rayquaza · #384, Jirachi · #385, Deoxys · #386

Sinnoh cosmology recounts the creation of the cosmos by the original deity Arceus at the Spear Pillar atop Mount Coronet. Unova history tells of an original dragon that split in two during a civil war between twin hero princes, dividing into Reshiram, dragon of truth, and Zekrom, dragon of ideals, with the frozen husk Kyurem remaining in the Giant Chasm. Defending the wild habitats are the Swords of Justice led by Cobalion, the Forces of Nature trio, and mythical guardians Victini, Meloetta, and Genesect.

Kalos myth revolves around the cycle of life and destruction embodied by the life-giving stag Xerneas and the death cocoon Yveltal, both tied to the energy of an ancient ultimate weapon. Alola's tropical islands are guarded by the four natural land spirits known as the Tapu. In-Universe Game Canon

Source: Core series dialogue, trainer histories, and official geography maps from Game Freak releases.

Regions and Spatial Geography

The first four regions (Kanto, Johto, Hoenn, and Sinnoh) sit in close geographic proximity, based on the real-world Japanese regions of Kanto, Kansai, Kyushu, and Hokkaido. Kanto and Johto share a contiguous land border connected by Route 27, Tohjo Falls, and the Magnet Train between Saffron City and Goldenrod City. Later titles moved to overseas territories: Unova is based on the New York metropolitan area, Kalos on northern France, Alola on Hawaii, Galar on the United Kingdom, and Paldea on the Iberian Peninsula. For regional borders, native dex slices, and field survey maps from Kanto #001 to Paldea #1025, see the Regions index.

Main Series Event Cross-Referencing

How different regional adventures correlate across the core continuity:

  • Kanto and Hoenn: Red disbands Team Rocket in Kanto at roughly the same time Brendan or May confronts Team Magma and Team Aqua across Hoenn.
  • Johto and Sinnoh: Three years later, Red trains atop Mt. Silver during the Johto league challenge, while Lucas or Dawn resolves the space-time rift created by Team Galactic in Sinnoh.
  • Unova through Paldea: Several years elapse before Black and White, followed two years later by Black 2 and White 2. Subsequent journeys across Kalos, Alola, Galar, and Paldea unfold chronologically forward in modern sequence.

Anomalous Spacetime Phenomena

The game universe features specific localized breaches in space and time:

  • Space-time rifts: Atmospheric tears over Mount Coronet in ancient Hisui, triggered by Giratina destabilizing the Temple of Sinnoh, which caused items and people from different centuries to fall through.
  • The Distortion World: A parallel plane where Giratina was banished. Normal gravity, surface orientation, and the standard forward flow of time do not apply there.
  • Ultra Wormholes: Interdimensional gateways in Alola opened by Solgaleo, Lunala, or Necrozma. These tunnels connect our world to extradimensional habitats such as Ultra Deep Sea and the ruined civilization of Ultra Megalopolis.
  • Area Zero and Paradox Pokémon: The Great Crater of Paldea houses a functioning time machine built by Professor Sada or Professor Turo. The machine extracted ancient past or distant future Paradox specimens into the present day.

Release history

From Game Boy to Switch

The real-world development of the franchise spans thirty years of hardware changes:

  • 1996: Game Freak and Nintendo released Pocket Monsters Red and Green for the Game Boy in Japan, directed by Satoshi Tajiri with artwork by Ken Sugimori. This established the initial 151 species indexed on this site.
  • 1998-1999: International localized releases as Pokémon Red and Blue established the global franchise.
  • Hardware generations: The series transitioned across Game Boy Color (1999, Gen II), Game Boy Advance (2002, Gen III), Nintendo DS (2006, Gen IV), Nintendo 3DS (2013, Gen VI), and Nintendo Switch (2019, Gen VIII).
  • Data compatibility: The 2002 release of Ruby and Sapphire rebuilt the core internal data structures for individual Pokémon (introducing 31-point Individual Values and personality values). Because of this unified structure, a Pokémon caught on a Game Boy Advance cartridge in 2002 can still be transferred upward through the DS, 3DS, and Pokémon Bank into Pokémon HOME on the Nintendo Switch today.
